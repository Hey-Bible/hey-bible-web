Every morning at 7 Eastern we post one short clip: a landscape, a little music, and a verse. No sermon.

The first version was the simple one. The picture and the song carried the post. The verse sat in the caption, under the video.

![A big oak in autumn colour in a sunlit meadow, with no words on it](/img/blog/daily-verse-videos-before.jpg)

*The oak, before the card.*

We kept that quiet opening. The next step was to put the verse on the film as well, so you can read it with the sound off.

## The picture still opens the clip

Every clip still starts with only the landscape, for about half a second. Then a card flips in toward you. It rotates up into place, like a page turning up from the horizon. On it:

- the reference in gold, with the translation beside it
- a thin gold rule
- the verse in warm cream
- the translation's copyright line, small, underneath
- a dark plate you can see through, so the landscape stays in the picture

And in the corner, small and quiet: **@heybibleapp**.

A solid box would cover the meadow. The plate is dark enough for the type, and clear enough that the landscape stays in the frame.

We tried this first on Philippians 4:13, once wide and once tall. Every morning is those two cuts of one scene. Wide for some feeds, tall for others. New daily videos use that pattern. Clips that were already approved we left as they were.

## Words go on last

You can ask an image model to paint the verse into the sky. The letters often come back wrong. With Scripture, a near miss is the wrong verse.

So we generate the picture first, then set the type with real fonts, the way a film gets its titles.

A few other choices settled the same way:

- The song matches the still. A sunrise meadow gets guitar. A woods path gets unhurried guitar. The oak in this post uses sunny fingerstyle guitar and light piano, in a major key.
- The motion stays slow and even. The first fifteen seconds and the ten we added later move as one shot.
- One verse a day, at 7 AM Eastern. We make the clips at night.

[Season 2 of the podcast](/blog/behind-the-scenes-season-2) is a different look on purpose. Those chapter films are oil paintings and Ken Burns. The daily posts are photoreal morning light, made to sit in a social feed.

## The same card, in the app

The video card gave us a reason to restyle verse images in the app. Those images had carried the verse on a wide white band with black type. We wanted the videos and the in-app pictures to share one face.

We filed [issue #260](https://github.com/Hey-Bible/hey-bible-app/issues/260) and opened [PR #261](https://github.com/Hey-Bible/hey-bible-app/pull/261) so generated images could use the card from the videos: dark plate, gold reference, cream verse, gold rule, the same small watermark. We measured the plate's width and position off stills from the videos. A still cannot flip. It can use the same plate, type, and colours.

That pull request merged the next day.

If you follow [@heybibleapp](https://instagram.com/heybibleapp), new posts use the card. New verse images in the app use it too.

## Since then

*Added November 16.*

We lengthened the clips. They had been fifteen seconds. We added ten more so the picture and the song had time to settle. The second half moves at the same pace as the first.

We also changed the music. We pick the song from the still, after we can see it, on a newer model than the first beds.

We simplified the type. The verse is one cream line. We removed a gray copy that had sat under some of the letters.

Hey Bible's default verse images are now storybook sticker art on a cream page. Sticker art uses a cream plate. Photo-like styles keep the dark card from the videos.

You can move the text on a verse image and restyle it yourself.
