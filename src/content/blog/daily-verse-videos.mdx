Every morning at 7 Eastern we post one short clip: a landscape, a little music, and a verse. A little of God's word, enough to remember that he is good, that he cares for you, and that he is fighting for you.

This is how those clips took shape.

## A landscape and a song

They began as the simple version. The picture and the song carried the post. The verse sat in the caption, under the video.

![A big oak in autumn colour in a sunlit meadow, with no words on it](/img/blog/daily-verse-videos-before.jpg)

*The oak, before the card.*

We kept that quiet opening. Every clip still starts with only the landscape, for about half a second. Long enough to see where you are.

## The verse on the film

The next step was to put the verse on the film as well, so you can read it with the sound off.

*The same oak, with Psalm 118:24 on the card. Turn the sound on for the guitar.*

After that half second, a card flips in toward you. It rotates up into place, like a page turning up from the horizon. On it:

- the reference in gold, with the translation beside it
- a thin gold rule
- the verse in warm cream
- the translation's copyright line, small, underneath
- a dark plate you can see through, so the landscape stays in the picture

And in the corner, small and quiet: **@heybibleapp**.

The plate is dark enough for the type, and clear enough that the landscape stays in the frame.

Image models still struggle with text. They misspell words, smudge letters, or set the type in a font we would not use. So we generate the picture first, then set the type with real fonts, the way a film gets its titles.

We tried the card first on Philippians 4:13, once wide and once tall. Every morning is those two cuts of one scene. The wide cut goes to Threads and Facebook. The tall cut goes to X, Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok.

## The same card, in the app

The video card gave us a reason to restyle verse images in the app. Those images had carried the verse on a wide white band with black type. We wanted the videos and the in-app pictures to share one face.

We filed [issue #260](https://github.com/Hey-Bible/hey-bible-app/issues/260) and opened [PR #261](https://github.com/Hey-Bible/hey-bible-app/pull/261) so generated images could use the card from the videos: dark plate, gold reference, cream verse, gold rule, the same small watermark. We measured the plate's width and position off stills from the videos. A still cannot flip. It can use the same plate, type, and colours.

That pull request merged the next day, August 16.

## A little more time

The clips had been fifteen seconds. We added ten more so the picture and the song had time to settle. The second half moves at the same pace as the first.

We also started choosing the song from the still, after we could see it, on a newer model than the first beds. A sunrise meadow gets guitar. A woods path gets unhurried guitar. The oak in this post uses sunny fingerstyle guitar and light piano, in a major key.

The type got simpler too. The verse is one cream line. We removed a gray copy that had sat under some of the letters.

## Where they are now

New morning videos use this card. Clips that were already approved we left as they were.

Hey Bible's default verse images are now storybook sticker art on a cream page. Sticker art uses a cream plate. Photo-like styles keep the dark card from the videos. You can move the text on a verse image and restyle it yourself.

If you follow [@heybibleapp](https://instagram.com/heybibleapp), the new posts use this card. New verse images in the app use it too.

[Season 2 of the podcast](/blog/behind-the-scenes-season-2) is a different film on purpose. Those chapter films are oil paintings and Ken Burns. The morning posts stay photoreal, one verse a day, made the night before so you wake up to them.
